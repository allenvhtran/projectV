## Setup

**5.** The job is mostly waiting. You sit with a map table and a sighting instrument, and when you see smoke you take a bearing on it and call it in. Two towers with two bearings give you a location. That's the whole system.

**6.** It only works if people answer. Ellis Creek checked in twice a day on the radio — eight in the morning, six at night. Weather, visibility, any smoke. The log has a column for it, and a column beside that for which tower confirmed the report.

**7.** The 1994 entries are signed with initials. D.R. Nothing else — no full name anywhere in the book. The early ones are exactly what you'd expect. Clear. Haze to the southwest. Nothing to report. Nothing to report.

**8.** Then on the twenty-ninth of June, a dry lightning storm came through the basin. Forty strikes in two hours. It started three small fires and it took out the radio repeater on the next ridge over.

## Escalation

**10.** Which is the correct procedure, and it's also the last completely ordinary thing in the book. Because on the second of July, at the eighteen-hundred check-in, he logs a light.

## Turn

**18.** **[hero]** And then he writes the sentence that changes what the whole book is. The repeater is working. It has been working the entire time. There was never any blackout on their end. Only on his.

**19.** Read that again the way he must have. Nineteen days of check-ins. Weather, visibility, smoke, twice a day, every day, on schedule. And the equipment was fine. He'd been transmitting into perfect working order and nobody had answered once.

## Resolution

**30.** Which would make the log a hoax. Somebody's idea of a joke, left in an empty building for a quarter of a century. That's the reasonable answer and I want to give it to you. I can't, quite.

**31.** Because the fire observations in that book are correct. Every fire in that basin in the summer of 1994 is in there — the right day, the right bearing, the right time of first sighting. Cross-checked against the district's own records.

> *shot:* A column of smoke rising from dense forest in the far distance seen through heat haze, hazy orange-grey sky, wide flat landscape, no foreground detail

**32.** Three of them were spotted by Ellis Creek before any other tower called them in. You can't fake that after the fact any more accurately than the people who were actually watching. And you can't fake it in advance at all.

**33.** So that's what we have. A summer nobody worked, recorded accurately, twice a day, on schedule, by somebody who kept the neatest handwriting in the district. That's the answer. I don't think it helps.
